# Quality Bar: Deep-Sea Competition Dossier

- The shark is an immediately legible, real focal subject rather than a decorative silhouette.
- Black and warning yellow commit across structure and controls; aqua is reserved for learning state feedback.
- Information reads like a competition result board: direct labels, exact progress, strong alignment, no invented metrics.
- Frames use thin technical rules and compact spacing without reducing child-readable type or touch targets.
- Review screens become quieter work areas while retaining the same title voice, palette, focus treatment, and control grammar.
- Motion is limited to meaningful progress and task-state transitions.
- The surface fails this bar if it becomes a generic rounded-card checklist, hides the shark, or requires an adult to explain the next action.
